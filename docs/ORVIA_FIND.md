# ORVIA Find and Price Guard

Natural-language product discovery, image search, comparison, saved searches and price tracking. Everything shown is derived from the live catalogue and from prices Orvia itself observed. Status labels: **implemented**, **requires external credentials**, **planned**.

## How matching works

1. **Intent parser** (`intent.ts`, pure, no IO, `now` injected). Extracts, using regular expressions and allow-lists only:
   - budget (`under $40`, `below 30 dollars`, `₹2000`, `between $20 and $40`, `2k inr`; units such as "5 years" or "2 kg" are never read as money). `$` follows the market (USD/CAD), or USD in India; an explicit `CAD`, `USD`, `INR`, `₹` wins. Cross-currency budgets are converted at the platform FX rate (`fxPerUsd`) and flagged as converted. The budget applies to the **item price before shipping**.
   - delivery deadline (`before Friday`, `by next Monday`, `within 3 days`, `by 20 november`, `before christmas`, ISO dates) computed from `now` in the customer's country timezone (US New York, CA Toronto, IN Kolkata; one representative zone per country). `before X` means strictly before (the day before X). Past dates are ignored with a note.
   - need (mapped to `NEEDS`), sub-category (mapped to `CATEGORY_TREE`), audience (pet/kids, species, age), gift/recipient/occasion, attributes (material, size, colour), sort intent (`cheapest`, `best value`, `top rated`, `fastest`, `popular`) and comparison intent.
   - everything left over becomes keywords. Control characters and markup are stripped, length is capped at 300.
2. **Optional AI refinement** (`aiRefine.ts`). If `GEMINI_API_KEY` is set and `useAI` is not false, the query (fenced as untrusted) is sent to the AI service with a structured-output schema and a 4 s timeout. The AI may only add related search terms, need/category slugs from our own lists, a sort that the wording supports, and a budget number that literally appears in the query. It can never return products, prices, stock or claims. Any timeout, error or invalid output returns the deterministic parse unchanged (`engine.ai = "unavailable_fallback"`). The query text leaves our servers only in this step.
3. **Ranking** (`ranking.ts`, pure) over the real catalogue: sellable state, imaged, enabled and in stock in the country (cached 30 s per country, max 3000 products). Score = need/category/attribute hits + lexical match (title > tags > attributes > category > description) with synonym groups and a one-edit typo tolerance + TF-IDF cosine similarity + small real-signal terms (smoothed rating, units sold, trend). Relevance gate: at least half of the keywords, or a need/category/similarity hit.
4. **Hard filters**: budget, minimum rating (only counts products with at least 3 reviews), deadline, and a child-safety rule (a toy whose listed minimum age is above the child's age is never shown).
5. **`whyMatched`** is built from the matched field or applied filter (for example "Matches “dog” in the title", "material: rubber", "within your $40 budget (item price, before shipping)"). Nothing is generated by a model.
6. Sorting: `relevance`, `price_asc` (estimated delivered price), `rating` (products with fewer than 3 reviews last), `fastest`, `popular`, `value` = `0.5*relevance + 0.3*smoothedRating/5 + 0.2*cheapness` normalised within the result set (documented, deterministic).

### Semantic index

`SemanticIndex` (`semantic.ts`) is the seam for embeddings. The default for an embeddings provider is `NoopSemanticIndex` (returns nothing). The shipped in-process implementation is `TfIdfSemanticIndex`: genuine TF-IDF cosine similarity over product text. It matches shared vocabulary, not meaning, and the API says so (`engine.semantic`). A provider-backed implementation (embeddings API plus a vector store) would need credentials and is **planned**, not shipped.

## Honesty rules

- **Delivery**: each product's own `minDays`/`maxDays` window is compared with the customer's date. If even the earliest estimate is after the date the product is excluded (counted in `excluded.tooLate`); if only the latest estimate is after it, the product is shown with "May not arrive in time"; if the window is unknown it is flagged "Delivery time not available". An "ok" is an estimate, never a promise. Carrier cut-offs, weekends and customs are not modelled.
- **Delivered price** = item price + the country's standard shipping fee for a single item (zero above the free-shipping threshold), before tax/duty. It is an estimate.
- No supplier cost, margin or supplier identity is ever returned (tests assert this).
- Ratings shown or compared only with at least 3 reviews; otherwise "not enough reviews".
- Comparison "best for" is one measured attribute (lowest delivered price, fastest estimated window, highest rating), omitted when tied or when data is missing. It is not an overall recommendation.
- Image search describes the photo as keywords and ranks by wording. It is not a visual similarity search and says so in the response notes. The upload is validated (JPEG/PNG/WebP, magic bytes, `MEDIA_MAX_BYTES`), processed in memory and never stored.
- A user query cannot change behaviour: it is only parsed into filters or keywords (see `tests/unit/find-intent.test.ts`, injection cases).

## Price Guard

- **Price history**: collection `find_price_snapshots`. The scheduled job `find_price_snapshot` (daily) writes a row per product and country only when price or availability changed since the last observation, at most once per UTC day; creating an alert triggers one on-demand snapshot (never an identical row). Rows expire after two years.
- **History display** (`GET /find/price-history/:id`): shown only when data exists, always with the observation count and first-observed date. "Lowest price in the last N days" is reported only over the span our snapshots actually cover (e.g. "the 11 days we have been tracking this price"). With fewer than two observations no statement is made. Reference/compare-at prices are never manufactured.
- **Alerts** (`find_price_alerts`, `/account/price-guard`, max 50 active per user, owner-only access, other users get 404):
  - price drop of at least `dropPercent` (default 5%) versus the price when tracking began;
  - target price reached (target must be below the current price);
  - back in stock (only after we observed it out of stock);
  - better verified offer: another sellable product in the same category and top category with no conflicting key attribute (material, size, capacity, colour, type, age range, pack size), at least 3 reviews on both, rating within 0.3, and at least 5% lower estimated delivered price.
  Each condition fires once: dedupe state is stored on the alert (last notified price, last better offer) and notifications carry deterministic `dedupeKey`s, so re-running the job never double-sends. A price that recovers above the starting price re-arms drop alerts.
- **Consent**: alerts are only created by a signed-in user for themselves and are only emailed to that account, and only when the account email is verified and the account is not disabled. Unverified accounts see a notice and no email is sent; the alert state is not advanced, so it can still fire after verification. Each message states why it was sent. Template: `price_alert` (additive, `packages/notifications`).
- **Saved searches** (max 25 per user): optional daily "new matches" email (at most once a day, only products not seen before, dedupe key per search and day).
- Schedules (`FIND_SCHEDULES`): `find_price_snapshot` daily, `find_price_alerts` hourly (500 alerts per run, oldest-checked first), `find_saved_search_alerts` daily.

## Limits and rate limits

`POST /find` 30/min per client, `/find/image` 8/min, `/compare` 60/min, query 300 chars, 2-4 products to compare, 48 results max, catalogue cache 3000 products per country (above that scale use a real search engine), 50 price alerts and 25 saved searches per user.
